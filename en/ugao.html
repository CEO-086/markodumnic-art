A test, not part of the site

Viewing angle

The same canvas, two states. With the relief switched off it is a flat picture on the wall — which is how the exhibition room works now. With it switched on, light travels across the ridges of paint and the canvas changes as you move around it.

What you see here, and what you do not.

The test uses “Aria di Torino” (2024, oil on canvas, 60 × 50 cm). The relief is derived from a photograph of the work — calculated from the brightness and density of the strokes, not measured on the canvas. It shows the mechanism, not the final look.

With a proper capture the relief is measured: the camera stays still on a tripod while the light is moved around the canvas, for some thirty shots. The differences in the shadows give the true height of every stroke. Only then does this look like paint, rather than a similar effect.

Everything stays static — three images per work, no server.
